Dear Sir or Madam,

I am a member of a small neighborhood group in Port Verity that organizes a peaceful candlelight vigil every spring. For the past four years we have gathered in Wren Hollow Park without any trouble. This year, when we applied for the routine assembly permit, the parks office denied it, stating that our "message could provoke controversy" and that we would need to pay a $1,200 "security deposit" that was never required before.

When I asked which other groups had been charged this deposit, the clerk would not answer. A separate group that held a rally in the same park two weeks earlier, on a topic the office apparently found less objectionable, was not charged anything. It appears to me that the fee and the denial are based on the content of what we plan to say, not on any real safety concern.

I have the written denial, our four prior approved permits for the same event, and the fee schedule the office posts publicly, which does not list any such deposit. Our vigil is scheduled for May 30, so we are working against a deadline.

Could someone review whether the city can condition our permit this way? I am happy to provide all of our paperwork. Please contact me by phone or email.
